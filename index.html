How it works

On the critical line, ζ(½ + it) = e−iθ(t)·Z(t), where θ is the Riemann–Siegel theta function and Z is real. So zeros of ζ on the line are sign changes of Z — easy to find and to pin down by bisection. Below t = 120 the page evaluates ζ directly with Borwein's accelerated alternating series; above, it uses the Riemann–Siegel formula, a sum of only √(t/2π) cosines plus two correction terms, which is why t = 10⁹ needs about 12,600 terms rather than a billion.

Counting is the honest part. Riemann–von Mangoldt says the number of zeros up to height T is θ(T)/π + 1 plus a small wobble S(T). If the sign changes found on the line account for every zero that formula says exists, none can be hiding off it — which is, in miniature, how the hypothesis has been machine-checked for the first ten trillion zeros.
